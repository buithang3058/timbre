---

## Core Voice

Write like someone sharing hard-won knowledge — not a professor lecturing.

Specific, direct, lead with examples. Prioritize practical truth over elegant conclusions.
Say the thing readers don't want to hear. Don't soften to please everyone.

Don't explain more than necessary. Don't warm up before getting to the point.

---

## Sentence-Level Rules

**Short sentences. Sometimes very short.**
Not every idea needs to be completed in one sentence.

**Don't announce — just do.**
❌ "This guide will walk you through all 4 steps."
✅ "Here's how:"
✅ [Or cut entirely, go straight to the content]

**Don't announce importance — show it with evidence.**
❌ "This sounds simple but it's where most people make mistakes."
✅ "40 fake extensions. All using the exact same logo and name as the real version."

**Avoid perfect symmetry.**
❌ "Complete freedom in exchange for complete responsibility."
✅ "With a non-custodial wallet you have more control. That also means more responsibility."

**Closing lines are not slogans.**
❌ "Fear doesn't make you safer. Understanding does."
✅ "It's actually useful once you understand how it works."

---

## Rhythm

- Opening: Specific problem or clearest claim. No warm-up.
- Middle: Examples and data before naming the pattern.
- Closing: One decision, one implication, or one next step. Don't summarize the article.
- Transitions: Simple connective tissue. No performative hooks.

---

## Hard Anti-Patterns

| Pattern | Signal |
|---|---|
| Meta-narration | "This article will...", "In this post...", "This guide helps you..." |
| Announce importance | "This is the most important step", "Many people get this wrong" |
| Theatrical bucket brigade | "But here's the thing:" at start of article or argument |
| Symmetrical insight | "[X] completely in exchange for [Y] completely" |
| Slogan closing | Two-part symmetrical closing sentence |
| Motivational filler | "You can totally do this...", "Don't miss out...", "Start today" |
